Do you need German?

Why so few?

International companies and start-ups mostly hire through LinkedIn, StepStone or their own career pages, which can't be included here. The Bundesagentur's job board is used mostly by German employers, and most of them write their postings in German.

So read this as “on the largest public job board, English-only Werkstudent jobs are rare”, not as a figure for the whole market. The postings that do qualify are listed below.

Method & source

Data

Every morning a script collects all postings for “Werkstudent”, “Working Student” and “Werkstudierende” in Germany from the public job search of the Bundesagentur für Arbeit. Only postings with one of these words in the title are kept.

Job texts are analysed but never stored in public or shown here: they belong to the employers. This page only shows aggregated numbers, titles, company names and links to the original postings.

Not included

Postings that exist only on LinkedIn, StepStone, Indeed or company career pages. Large international employers are therefore underrepresented, especially for English-speaking roles.

How the numbers are made

  • Pay comes from the posting's salary fields or from phrases like “17,50 € brutto pro Stunde”. Each role (company + title) counts once, so an employer posting the same role in 97 cities doesn't dominate. A median needs at least 10 roles from 5 employers.
  • German is required for phrases like “fließend Deutsch” or “German C1”, a plus for “von Vorteil” or “nice to have”, and not needed for “English only” or English postings that never ask for it. German postings that don't mention it are counted as needing German.
  • Skills are matched against a dictionary of ~180 skills with German and English synonyms. Broad terms like “Vertrieb” only count next to words like “Kenntnisse” or “experience”.

These are rules, not a human reading every posting. They were checked by hand on samples of real postings and are covered by tests. Code and data: github.com/morty1338/werkstudent-radar.
